VMware Subscription Commerce Cloud

Problem
VMware was moving from perpetual licenses to subscriptions, and enterprises, partners and MSPs had no self-service way to order subscriptions or migrate.
Platform scope
A subscription commerce platform covers the whole customer lifecycle: an offer and price catalog; self-service quoting and ordering; subscription creation, provisioning and entitlements; payment methods and billing; usage tracking; and renewals, upgrades and migrations. Each segment has different needs: enterprises want self-service ordering, partners need to price and order on behalf of customers, and MSPs often buy on usage-based terms.
Approach
Launched the platform from inception and owned the roadmap and business requirements for 50+ platform services across that lifecycle: offers and pricing, subscription creation and provisioning, support, payment methods, billing, usage analytics and renewals. Worked with R&D on access and entitlement design.
Result
$10M+ ARR, 35% operational efficiency gain, and the VMware CIO Award FY23 plus two more internal awards for the upgrade programs.

Deal Desk AI assistant and Subscription Upgrade

Problem
Quote lines drifted out of sync with Oracle Install Base data, and applying SnS credit and ending install base instances for upgraded customers was manual.
Context
In quote-to-cash, Deal Desk checks quotes, discounts and terms before approval. For renewals and upgrades, every quote line must match the customer’s Install Base and service contract (status, quantity, Support and Subscription (SnS) number, end dates); when they drift, orders are rejected or reworked. Approvals normally follow a tiered matrix: low-value quotes with standard discounts are auto-approved, mid-range amounts or discounts go to the Regional Director of Sales, and the highest amounts or deepest discounts go to the VP of Sales. For brownfield customers moving from perpetual licenses to subscriptions, the remaining SnS value is credited and the legacy lines and install base instances are ended.
Why standard CPQ was not enough
Standard Salesforce CPQ setup (product rules, price rules, discount schedules and approvals) works on data held inside Salesforce. It left gaps for Deal Desk:
  • No live check against Install Base: rules could not compare each quote line with current Oracle Install Base and service contract records, so mismatches surfaced only after an order failed.
  • Approvals route, they do not review: the approval matrix sends a quote to auto-approval, the Regional Director or the VP of Sales by amount and discount, but it does not tell Deal Desk which lines are wrong or why.
  • Upgrade and renewal quotes are line-heavy: checking many lines by hand was slow and error-prone, so an assistant that reviews them before approval removes that work.
Approach
Created a real-time Quote Lines Validator and AI assistant for Deal Desk in Salesforce CPQ, so quotes are checked before they enter the approval matrix and approvers and auto-approval work on clean quotes. Also automated SnS credit calculation and install base termination, working with the Deal Desk, customer service operations and Finance teams.
Result
40% shorter quote-to-approval cycle, fewer manual line-by-line steps for brownfield upgrades.

Tiered and bundled benefit plans in Salesforce CPQ

Problem
TriNet charged a flat per employee per month (PEPM) price for every worksite employee (WSE), regardless of company size. Customer feedback and TriNet’s strategy to grow revenue called for pricing that rewards larger customers and encourages broader purchase.
Context
Salesforce CPQ models what is sold as a product catalog with bundles and options, applies price books, price rules and discount schedules, routes exceptions through approval, and turns an accepted quote into an order and contract that downstream billing can use. Volume tiers and pre-configured bundles are standard CPQ pricing patterns, and they only work when the catalog and rules are clean enough for sales to trust.
Approach
Designed the pricing model and configured it in Salesforce CPQ:
  • Tiered pricing: the price per employee steps down as the customer’s WSE count grows, in place of one flat rate.
  • Bundled pricing: pre-configured offers priced at a discount compared with buying the same plans as standard offers.
  • Rules: pricing and discount rules so each quote carries consistent, approved pricing into the order.
Result
40% better Lead-to-Cash execution efficiency, with a pricing structure that supports TriNet’s revenue growth strategy and gives customers a clearer reward for scale.

Benefits rating and carrier platform at TriNet

Problem
Premiums relied on coarse legacy band codes, and ~20 carriers each exchanged enrollment and member data through separate EDI and SFTP feeds with frequent manual fixes.
Context
A benefits rate engine calculates premiums from plan rates, risk factors and geography. Moving from band codes to rates, risk modifiers and national and regional factors lets pricing reflect the actual risk-sharing or guaranteed-cost terms with each carrier. Each carrier receives enrollment changes as HIPAA 834 files over SFTP and returns member ID feeds, so onboarding a carrier means agreeing the file layout, mapping fields, testing, going live and reconciling exceptions.
Approach
Modernized the Rate Engine for risk-sharing and guaranteed-cost carriers; onboarded carriers on HIPAA 834 files and member ID feeds; leading phased consolidation to a single provider to retire feeds.
Result
Pricing accuracy improved to 95%, 95% sync accuracy and 80% fewer manual errors, with consolidation reducing the number of feeds to maintain.

AI-assisted customer service operations

Problem
Customer Service Operations (CSO) already used RPA bots for repeatable case handling, but the bots run on a schedule. Cases that arrived after a run, hit data or processing errors, or needed human judgment waited, so resolution was slow and costly.
How the two work together
The RPA bots run in the early hours of each day and pick up every open service case created the previous day or earlier. The Generative AI assistant works as a co-pilot for the CSO team in Salesforce Service Cloud. It reviews cases the bots have not yet picked up and cases that need manual review, and summarizes the details. It suggests the next action, or passes the case to the right business team’s queue or to a business manager, so the team can resolve it quickly.
Approach
Delivered the assistant as a hybrid extension to the existing RPA bots rather than a replacement, so each case is handled by the bots, the co-pilot or the team, depending on what it needs.
Why a co-pilot helps
Generative AI is widely used in service operations for the same reasons:
  • Faster case review: summaries replace reading long case histories, so agents move to the next step sooner.
  • Fewer stuck cases: cases with data or error issues, or ones the bots have not reached, are surfaced for review instead of waiting for the next run.
  • Faster hand-offs: suggested next actions and routing to the right queue or manager cut back-and-forth, leaving experienced staff free for cases that need judgment.
Result
90% faster case resolution and 30% lower operating cost.
