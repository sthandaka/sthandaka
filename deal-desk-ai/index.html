Deal Desk AI assistant and real-time quote validation

Role: Product Manager. Domain: subscription quoting and approvals in a CPQ-to-order flow.

40% shorter quote-to-approval cycle, with fewer manual line-by-line steps on upgrade quotes.

Problem

An enterprise software company was moving customers from perpetual licenses to subscriptions. Quote lines drifted out of sync with install base data in the ERP, so errors surfaced late, at order or billing. For customers converting a license with an active support contract, the support credit and the install base termination were handled by hand. Deal Desk checked quotes line by line, which slowed approvals and caused rework.

Approach

I paired a real-time quote validator with an AI assistant for Deal Desk, built into the CPQ quote screen. The principle: code does the exact work, and AI explains and guides.

  • Validator (rules): checks lines against install base, product rules, the price book and term rules as the quote is edited.
  • Credit and termination (code): support credit is prorated on remaining days, and the old install base instance is ended on conversion.
  • Assistant (AI): explains why a line failed, suggests a fix, gives discount guidance within policy and summarizes the deal for approvers.
  • Approval: stays with people. The assistant never approves a discount.

How a quote flows

  1. A seller or customer builds a quote in CPQ.
  2. The validator checks changed lines in real time and flags blocks, warnings and notes.
  3. The assistant explains each flag and proposes a fix.
  4. The quote passes credit check and approval, with a short deal summary for approvers.
  5. The approved quote becomes an order, a subscription and an invoice.

Options considered

OptionStrengthGap
Rules-only validatorExact and auditableError codes alone did not help sellers
AI-only assistantHelpful and flexibleRisky for pricing and credit math
Hybrid (chosen)Exact checks plus clear guidanceRules need an owner and upkeep

Results

  • 40% shorter quote-to-approval cycle.
  • Exceptions-only review on upgrade quotes instead of checking every line.
  • Errors caught at quote time rather than at order or billing.

What I learned

  • Fix data drift at the source, and show data freshness instead of hiding it.
  • Severity levels (block, warn, note) cut false-positive fatigue.
  • Generating explanations only from rule output keeps the AI consistent with the rules.
  • Shadow mode and override tracking built trust with Deal Desk.
